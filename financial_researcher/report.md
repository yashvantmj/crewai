## 1. Executive Summary

Sigma Lithium Corporation has evolved from a lithium development company into a commercial lithium producer. Its flagship asset, the **Grota do Cirilo** operation in Minas Gerais, Brazil, began production in **April 2023** and is now the foundation of the company’s growth strategy. The company’s Phase 1 Greentech Plant has an annualized nameplate capacity of approximately **270,000 tonnes per year** of lithium oxide concentrate, while the planned Phase 2 expansion is expected to add approximately **250,000 tonnes per year**, potentially increasing total annual capacity to around **520,000 tonnes**.

Sigma’s investment and strategic profile is defined by a combination of attractive growth potential and meaningful operational risk. On the positive side, the company has established commercial production, generated material revenue, reported competitive cost metrics, signed offtake agreements, and positioned itself as an ESG-oriented supplier of lithium concentrate to the global battery supply chain. Its reported **2Q25 CIF China cash operating cost of approximately US$442/t** and **AISC of approximately US$594/t** suggest a potentially resilient cost position relative to many lithium producers.

Financial performance has improved since the challenging lithium pricing environment of 2024. Reported figures include **FY2024 revenue of approximately US$151.4 million**, **1Q25 revenue of approximately US$47.7 million**, **4Q25 operating cash flow of approximately US$31 million**, and **1Q26 revenue of approximately US$48 million** with strong profitability indicators, including a **61% gross margin**, **39% EBITDA margin**, and **26% net profit margin**.

However, Sigma’s outlook is not without significant risks. The company remains exposed to volatile lithium prices, execution risk related to its Phase 2 expansion, balance-sheet and liquidity considerations, and operational concentration in a single core asset. Most importantly, in 2026 the company faced heightened regulatory scrutiny in Brazil after labor inspectors reportedly shut down three waste piles at its flagship mine and later fined the company for depositing waste in a pile that had been shut down. These developments are particularly important because Sigma’s brand is closely tied to sustainability and “green lithium” positioning.

Overall, Sigma Lithium is a high-potential but high-risk lithium producer. If the company can maintain low costs, resolve regulatory and waste-management issues, reduce debt, and execute Phase 2 successfully, it could become one of the most important lithium concentrate producers outside Australia and China. Conversely, prolonged regulatory problems, weaker lithium prices, or expansion delays could pressure margins, liquidity, and investor confidence.

## 2. Company Overview

### 2.1 Business Description

Sigma Lithium Corporation is focused on producing high-purity lithium concentrate from hard-rock spodumene deposits in Brazil. The company’s core business is supplying lithium-bearing concentrate to the battery materials supply chain, including refiners, cathode producers, battery manufacturers, and potentially automakers seeking diversified lithium supply.

Sigma markets its product under an environmentally differentiated brand, often described in company materials as **“Green Lithium”** or **“Quintuple Zero Green Lithium.”** This branding is tied to claims around reduced environmental impact, including lower carbon intensity, improved water efficiency, dry-stacked or reduced-impact tailings management, and broader responsible-mining practices.

### 2.2 Core Asset: Grota do Cirilo

Sigma’s principal operating asset is the **Grota do Cirilo** lithium operation in the Jequitinhonha Valley of Minas Gerais, Brazil. The project is one of the most prominent lithium operations in Brazil and is central to the country’s emergence as a more relevant jurisdiction in the global lithium supply chain.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Core asset | Grota do Cirilo |
| Location | Minas Gerais, Brazil |
| Commodity | Lithium-bearing spodumene concentrate |
| Initial production | April 2023 |
| Current operating stage | Commercial production |
| Phase 1 capacity | Approximately 270,000 tonnes per year |
| Planned Phase 2 additional capacity | Approximately 250,000 tonnes per year |
| Potential total capacity after Phase 2 | Approximately 520,000 tonnes per year |
| End market | Battery supply chain, electric vehicles, energy storage, refining customers |

The asset’s importance is amplified by its scale potential. With Phase 1 already operating and Phase 2 planned, Sigma has a pathway to become a much larger supplier of lithium concentrate.

## 4. Historical Performance

2023 was a transformative year for Sigma Lithium.

In 2026, Sigma reported strong profitability indicators but also faced important regulatory challenges.

- Gross margin of approximately **61%**.
- EBITDA margin of approximately **39%**.
- Net profit margin of approximately **26%**.

This regulatory issue is material because it directly affects operating risk, ESG credibility, and investor confidence.

## 6. Strategic Positioning

### 6.1 Role in the Lithium Supply Chain

Sigma produces lithium-bearing spodumene concentrate, which is typically processed further into lithium chemicals used in batteries. Its customers may include refiners, battery material companies, and other participants in the EV and energy storage supply chain.

The company benefits from exposure to long-term battery demand, but it is also exposed to the pricing dynamics of the spodumene concentrate market.

### 6.2 ESG and “Green Lithium” Positioning

Sigma has built much of its identity around environmentally differentiated lithium production. This has strategic value because battery and automotive customers increasingly focus on responsible sourcing, traceability, carbon footprint, water use, and social license.

However, this positioning also raises the reputational stakes. The 2026 waste-pile regulatory issue is particularly sensitive because it challenges the company’s sustainability narrative. To preserve the value of its ESG branding, Sigma will need to demonstrate transparent compliance, safe waste management, and credible third-party validation of its environmental claims.

### 6.3 Brazil as a Lithium Jurisdiction

Brazil is becoming more relevant in the global lithium market. Compared with traditional lithium supply centers such as Australia, Chile, Argentina, and China, Brazil offers potential supply diversification.

Advantages include:

- Established mining expertise.
- Hard-rock lithium potential.
- Atlantic shipping access.
- Potential appeal to North American and European buyers.
- Diversification away from dominant existing supply chains.

Sigma is one of the most visible lithium producers in Brazil, giving it strategic significance beyond its current production volumes.

Sigma’s 2025 cost data suggests that the company’s competitive position increasingly depends on its ability to maintain a low-cost operating model. ### 11.4 ESG Positioning Creates Both Upside and Scrutiny

Sigma’s sustainability-oriented branding is a commercial asset. Customers in the battery and automotive sectors increasingly value lower-impact supply. However, ESG branding also increases scrutiny. Any perceived gap between sustainability claims and operating practices can quickly become a reputational issue.

### 11.5 Phase 2 Is the Key Growth Catalyst

Sigma’s growth story is closely tied to Phase 2. The planned expansion could nearly double capacity and improve the company’s relevance to large customers. However, it also concentrates near-term execution risk.

The market is likely to judge Sigma on whether it can deliver Phase 2 while also reducing debt and resolving regulatory concerns.

## 12. Market Outlook

### 12.1 Lithium Market Context

The lithium market remains structurally attractive but cyclically volatile. Long-term demand is supported by electric vehicles, battery storage, and energy-transition policies. However, near-term pricing can be affected by:

- Inventory cycles.
- New mine supply.
- Chinese battery demand.
- EV sales growth rates.
- Refining capacity.
- Contract renegotiations.
- Macroeconomic conditions.
- Policy changes.

The lithium market has already shown that prices can move dramatically in both directions. Producers must therefore be judged not only on growth potential, but also on cost resilience and balance-sheet strength.

### 12.2 Outlook for Sigma Lithium

Sigma’s outlook depends on five central variables:

1. **Lithium prices**  
   A stronger lithium price environment would significantly improve Sigma’s revenue, margins, and cash flow. A weaker market would pressure returns and could slow Phase 2 development.

2. **Operating stability**  
   Continued production near Phase 1 nameplate capacity would support cash generation and customer confidence.

3. **Cost sustainability**  
   Maintaining low cash costs and AISC is essential to Sigma’s resilience.

4. **Regulatory resolution**  
   The waste-pile issue must be resolved credibly to protect operations and ESG reputation.

5. **Phase 2 execution**  
   Successful expansion could transform Sigma’s scale and market relevance.

## 15. Conclusion

Sigma Lithium has reached an important stage in its corporate development. It is no longer merely a lithium growth story based on future production expectations; it is now an operating producer with revenue, cash flow, customers, and a credible expansion plan.

The most attractive elements of the company are its Grota do Cirilo asset, potentially competitive cost structure, exposure to long-term battery demand, and Phase 2 expansion opportunity. If successful, Phase 2 could elevate Sigma into a larger and more strategically important lithium concentrate producer.

At the same time, the company faces substantial challenges. The 2026 waste-pile regulatory issue is a serious matter because it affects both operational risk and the credibility of Sigma’s sustainability-focused brand. Lithium price volatility remains another major uncertainty, and expansion execution will be critical.

In summary, Sigma Lithium is a promising but complex lithium producer. Its future performance will depend on whether management can convert current operating momentum into sustainable production, strong cash generation, reduced leverage, regulatory resolution, and disciplined growth. If the company succeeds, it could become one of the most important lithium producers outside the dominant Australian and Chinese supply chains. If it fails to resolve regulatory concerns or if lithium prices deteriorate significantly, its financial flexibility and growth plans could come under pressure.
